Neural engineer and PhD researcher at the interface of computational neurophysiology and clinical neurorehabilitation.
My research aims to move beyond describing brain activity to investigate neural responsiveness and integration, with a growing interest in personalised, adaptive stimulation guided by physiological state.

Research and professional experience

10/2024 - present
Doctoral researcher in BioRobotics
Scuola Superiore Sant'Anna / AIRLab, IRCCS Fondazione Don Carlo Gnocchi, Florence
My doctoral research investigates spontaneous brain dynamics and neural responses to external and internal stimuli, primarily in severe acquired brain injury and disorders of consciousness. I develop quantitative EEG methods to characterise brain states and their temporal organisation, and investigate how neural activity responds to, synchronises with and integrates sensory and bodily signals. Current work spans EEG microstate analysis, spontaneous blink dynamics, auditory and somatosensory responses, and heartbeat-related cortical activity, combining methodological development with experimental and clinical research.
Supervisors: C. M. Oddo and A. Mannini.

Selected ongoing research

Blink timing
Assessing whether spontaneous blink timing remains synchronised with predictable auditory stimuli after severe acquired brain injury.
Cardiac feedback
Investigating how passive cardiac biofeedback affects heartbeat-evoked potentials in healthy participants.
Auditory tagging
Investigating whether patterned auditory stimulation improves the characterisation of auditory steady-state responses in disorders of consciousness.
Touch and EEG
Assessing EEG responses to vibrotactile stimulation and the discriminability of stimulation parameters.
Clinical trials
Tracking longitudinal EEG changes during cognitive rehabilitation and robot-assisted verticalisation in patients with severe acquired brain injury.
